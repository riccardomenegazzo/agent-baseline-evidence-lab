# v0.13.0 — Customer Acceptance Envelope

v0.13.0 turns the versioned customer policy layer into a portable, independently verifiable
customer-specific acceptance artifact without changing the generic Customer Trust Handoff.

## Policy-neutral evidence, customer-specific acceptance

The Customer Trust Handoff remains reusable and policy-neutral. The new `abl-accept` command builds a
separate Customer Acceptance Envelope only after the selected versioned customer policy evaluates to
`PASS`.

This lets multiple customers evaluate the same evidence handoff against different requirements
without regenerating, relabeling or weakening the underlying evidence.

## Acceptance envelope

`abl-accept create` verifies the source handoff and its Ed25519 signature, extracts the Customer
Decision Brief by semantic role, recomputes the selected customer policy and fails closed if the
policy is not satisfied.

A successful envelope includes:

- the original Customer Trust Handoff and signature;
- the public verification key;
- the exact versioned customer policy;
- the recomputable policy evaluation and signature;
- a signed acceptance statement binding handoff, decision, policy and evaluation digests;
- a manifest covering all envelope members.

The final envelope ZIP is itself signed.

## Offline verification

`abl-accept verify` checks archive integrity, the nested handoff, nested handoff signature, policy
recomputation, policy-evaluation signature, acceptance-statement bindings/signature and the outer
envelope signature when supplied.

Tampering with the policy, decision linkage, evaluation, signed statements, nested handoff or final
package therefore invalidates the corresponding verification boundary.

## Release hardening

The clean-wheel release smoke test now exercises both `abl-policy` and `abl-accept` entrypoints so a
published release cannot silently omit the customer-policy or acceptance-envelope CLIs.

## Claims boundary

A Customer Acceptance Envelope is not production authorization or certification. `PASS` means only
that one verified evidence handoff satisfies one exact included acceptance policy. The design does
not convert policy into observed security evidence and does not introduce a synthetic trust score.
